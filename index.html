For collaborators and referrers

If someone tells you their business has stopped growing, or that they have lots of ideas, big ambitions and no plan, I am the person to send them to.

Everything you need to make that introduction is on this page. It takes about a minute. Nothing to download.

You do not need to diagnose anything

You just need to recognise something you have already heard someone say.

  1. “I’m too busy to work on the business.”

    Running between meetings, no protected time to think.

  2. “We grew to this point and then it just stopped.”

    The effort has not changed. The results have.

  3. “We know we want to expand, we just don’t know where to start.”

    Lots of ideas, real ambition, no structure to act on any of it.

  4. “I’ve been given a growth target and I’m not sure how to hit it.”

    A step-change in expectations, and no plan they are confident presenting upwards.

  5. “I need a new website.”

    Or LinkedIn, or social, or content, or thought leadership. Asking for a tactic when the problem sits upstream of it. Worth sending if they are open to being told why first.

  6. “We’re thinking about opening in Dubai.”

    Or Abu Dhabi, or Riyadh. A UK or US firm looking at the Gulf.

Who they usually are

Firms that sell expertise, where the leader has become the bottleneck. Partners, founders and practice heads rather than marketing teams. Usually law firms, often accountants and other professional firms, and it works anywhere someone brilliant at their craft is running a business they were never trained to run.

It also works for individual leaders inside larger firms: a Practice Head or Head of Department carrying a growth target for their own area, without a plan for hitting it.

From solo practitioners up to around 150 people, though complexity matters more than size.

I work with firms in the UK, the US and across the Gulf, both in their home market and when they are moving into a new one.

Who they are not

Retail, e-commerce, or anyone whose growth problem is a volume problem. Anyone who will not spend any time on the thinking: wanting a website is fine, and is often exactly the referral I want, but refusing the conversation about why is not. Pre-launch is fine, often ideal, because they are still in planning mode. Twelve months in with the runway spent is not, because there is nothing left to act on the plan with.
